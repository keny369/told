Why people callUsually a son or daughter, usually a bit late

Families rarely lose the dates. They lose the voice.

The version of events only one person could give. The way they told it. The detail nobody thought to write down because everyone assumed there would be time.

The call to Told tends to come after something has shifted. An eightieth birthday. A diagnosis. A move into care. A grandchild who will never meet the person as they were. The intention to record Mum or Dad has usually existed for years. What changes is that the window is now visibly closing.

Told exists to make the next step easy while it is still possible, and to do it properly, so that what comes back is something a family keeps.

Three ways to recordThe storyteller never operates anything

Someone else handles the technology. You choose who.

A person in their eighties should not have to learn software to be listened to. Told is built so that they sit down and talk. Everything else happens around them.

Told asks the questions

A patient interviewer that remembers.

A tablet on a stand. A calm face on the screen asks one question, listens, and picks up where the last conversation left off. Ten minutes today and forty tomorrow is fine. It doesn't get tired, doesn't watch the clock, and will happily hear the same story three times.

You ask, Told helps

Thirty minutes every Sunday.

A son, daughter or grandchild leads the conversation on their own phone. Told suggests the next question, notices the thread worth following, and organises what was recorded. Different family members can record different parts of a life over a few months.

We send an interviewer

For the conversations that matter most.

A trained interviewer visits. Told handles the preparation, the transcription and the follow-up questions for next time. Some material needs a person in the room who knows when to stop talking. Software is not a substitute for that.

For organisationsOne program, twenty lives, one invoice

Twenty residents. One purchase order.

An organisation funds the program. Its people tell their stories. Their families receive the record. With permission, selected stories become a collection that belongs to the community that made it.

Retirement living

Independent-living communities commission a defined group of residents. Told brings the interviewer or the stations. Residents bring a few photographs and sit down. The village gets a community project people talk about. Families get something they keep.

Cultural and migrant associations

A collection built around one experience: arriving from Vietnam in 1978, Greek Melbourne, post-war Italian families in the north. Interviews in the first language, with reviewed translation. Questions written with the community, not for it.

Clubs and societies

The players, coaches, volunteers and committee members who carry a club's history in their heads. Told records them and connects the accounts. Three people will remember 1973 differently. That's the point.

Companies and institutions

Founders, long-serving staff and the moments that shaped the place, recorded before that knowledge retires.

Common questionsThe ones people ask before they call

Before you ask us.

Does the person being recorded need to use any technology?

No. The storyteller sits down and talks. Family, staff or a Told interviewer handle setup, identity and permissions. Where Told's own interviewer is used, the storyteller sees a face on a tablet, hears one question at a time and can stop whenever they like.

Can stories be recorded in a language other than English?

Yes. If a story was lived in Vietnamese, Greek, Italian, Mandarin or another language it can be told in that language. The family receives the original recording and a translation that a person has checked.

Who can see the recordings?

Every story starts private. The storyteller decides, chapter by chapter, whether it stays with them, is shared with named family, is visible to the community that commissioned a collection, or is made public. Public use requires explicit written consent.

Does AI write the life story?

No. The original video and audio remain the authoritative record. Software transcribes, organises and suggests follow-up questions. A person reviews names, places and meaning before anything is treated as final.

How long does it take?

It depends on the person. Short conversations over several weeks work better than one long afternoon. Ten minutes today and forty tomorrow is fine. A typical life runs to several hours of recording gathered over a couple of months.

Do you work in residential aged care?

Residential aged care is a different setting from retirement living. Where a resident may not have full capacity to consent, Told works to a specific process agreed with the provider rather than a generic form. Talk to us first.

What does it cost?

Told quotes a plan and a price before anyone is asked a question. Cost depends on which of the three ways of recording you choose and, for organisations, how many people are in the collection. Email hello@told.media.
