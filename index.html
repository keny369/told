Why it usually doesn't happen

Two things stop families from ever doing this

1

It never gets finished

The memoir kit arrives. The first few questions get answered. By week six it's in a drawer, and everyone means to get back to it. Then the window closes.

2

It doesn't sound like them

The answers go through a machine and come out smooth. The family reads it and thinks: that's lovely, but Mom doesn't talk like that.

Told is built to remove both. It runs the conversations so nobody has to nag anyone, and it builds the story from their actual words, with the recording behind every quote and every fact, so you can check that it's them.

How it works

Six conversations. About six weeks. Fifteen minutes a week from you, and an hour at the end.

1

Set it up in ten minutes

Tell us who the story is about, the names and places they're likely to mention, and who in the family should be able to hear it. We send a code to their phone. Someone sets it up with them once, in person.

2

They talk

The first conversation asks for the story they always tell, then for the chapters of their life as they see them, and reads the list back until they say it's right. After that the app walks their chapters in their order, one question at a time, out loud and in big type, with one big button. They answer for as long as they like. Twenty to thirty minutes, once a week, six times. It knows when a chapter has enough in it and when to ask for the scene behind something they just said.

3

You check the names

After each conversation you get a short list of names and places to confirm, each with a few seconds of them saying it. About fifteen minutes. Once the first list is done, the first chapter is on the story page as a preview, so you can see what you're getting before the second conversation.

4

Read it. Hear it.

The story arrives as chapters, built from their own words with short connecting sentences. Tap any quote or any fact and hear the recording it came from. If they said "I think it was 1968", that is what the story says. Anything we can't tie to the recording is flagged for a person to look at, never smoothed over.

5

The book

Before we print, you check the names, dates and who's who against the recordings. That's the hour. When you say the story is them, we print it: a hardcover, with a small code beside each quote that plays their voice. Nothing is printed until you've read it and said yes.

Every reason this usually fails

And what we do about each one

They won't do it

The app asks, they answer, and nobody in the family has to nag. One question, one button, big type. Someone sets it up with them once, in person.

They forget

The app sets a reminder on their phone for the next conversation. Six conversations, about a week apart, and it ends. Nothing runs for a year.

They ramble

Good. The app asks for the scene behind the thing they just mentioned, and nothing they say is thrown away. What doesn't fit a chapter goes in its own section at the end.

There's something they don't want to talk about

They say so and the app moves on. It's never asked again. The hard parts come last, once they're used to it, and they can stop any time.

They say something private

They decide who can hear it, from their phone, and can change their mind. They can leave anything out, and they can delete everything. The family manages the story within what they've allowed, not beyond it. They also choose what happens to it after they're gone.

Something happens to them halfway

Everything recorded so far is kept, in full, within what they allowed. We build the story from what exists, and the book can still be printed.

It sounds like a machine wrote it

It's assembled from their words. The connecting sentences are short and plain, every quote and every fact points to the recording it came from, and anything we can't tie to the recording is flagged for a person. You check the names, dates and who's who against the audio before we print. We don't produce polished narratives, because polish is where people stop sounding like themselves.

We lose it

Every recording is kept lossless, in Sydney. You can download all of it at any time, and the download works without us. The story page is included for five years; after that, a small yearly fee, or simply keep your copy.

Can I add photos?

Not yet. The first edition is their words and their voice. Photos are coming.

Can six conversations hold a whole life?

No, and we don't pretend they can. What you get is a portrait: the shape of their life in the chapters they chose, the scenes told properly, their people, their sayings, and what they wanted kept. The book says which chapters were covered and lists the ones left open at the back, so nobody wonders. Shorter packages, one story about their parents, one trip, one lesson, come later.
